## 2026-09-08 — Emotional spine and first journey

- Named protagonist: James, seeking escape into a fantasy world where he has meaningful agency over his life.
- Entry decision: on arrival, James should immediately understand that he can continue a previous game or start a new one.
- Relationship model: play should feel like talking to a Dungeon Master at a real tabletop with friends, not issuing commands to software.
- Submission emotion: after James presses Enter, waiting should feel fun, exciting, and possibly nonsensical; the intended meaning of “nonsensical” remains open.
- Natural-language trust test: equivalent everyday phrasings must work without MUD command syntax. `go north` and `walk north` should both be understood.
- Agency trust test: improvised intentions such as `slap him` or `pour a beer over his head` should produce credible world action and consequences, even when the action was not pre-authored.
- Anti-pattern: never feel like a railroaded experience constrained to a programmed menu of actions and outcomes.
- Readability: the text-heavy experience must be easy to read and easy to skim.
- New UX goal: make the Dungeon Master relationship explicit while keeping narration, NPC dialogue, rules/mechanics, awards, and the LitRPG System perceptibly distinct.
- Visual metaphor: a living tabletop notebook.
- Dungeon Master: a visible, personable presence rather than an anonymous system voice.
- Waiting behavior: latency becomes playful anticipation through an animated activity and changing absurd backstage messages such as “Corralling the goblins”; never leave James staring at a disabled composer with no meaningful feedback.
- Typography prohibition: never use Comic Sans.
- Requested exploration: show alternatives for distinguishing Dungeon Master, narration, NPC dialogue, mechanics, and System/achievement voices.

## 2026-09-08 — Information-boundary correction

- The “On this page” summary is rejected; its items lack a clear player purpose.
- The persistent side presentation is limited to the player's own character for the first pass.
- The Dungeon Master does not receive persistent portrait space while the player character does not.

- Provisionally retained from the mockup: notebook metaphor, readable/skimmable long-form treatment, playful waiting feedback, and distinct presentation for narration, NPC speech, mechanics, player intention, and System notices.

## 2026-09-08 — Continue and new-game expectations

- Continue-game recall is conversational: the player may ask the Dungeon Master anything, and the answer is bounded by what the player's character legitimately knows.
- Dungeon Master answers must preserve the same information boundary as the rest of the UI and never reveal hidden simulation state.
- The interaction model must support both character intentions and table-style questions to the Dungeon Master in natural language.
- Starting a new game requires creating a new character.
- Character creation includes setting stats and supplying a background or character concept.
- Character creation is collaborative: the player works with the Dungeon Master to turn their concept into a valid playable character.
- Inherited rule: asking the Dungeon Master about already-known information is a zero-fictional-time inspection; typing, reading, model latency, and menus also do not advance world time.
- Inherited distinction: actual in-world speech or action may advance fictional time; out-of-character Dungeon Master questions and known-information inspection do not.
- Open: how the interface distinguishes a Dungeon Master question from an in-world intention without requiring command syntax.
- Open: exact character-creation rules, stat assignment method, validation boundaries, and completion criteria.
- Source reconciliation: the GDD intentionally has no defined character-creation flow or initial stat-assignment method, so collaborative creation is a new decision rather than a conflict.
- Inherited character constraints: no classes; universal basic skills remain attemptable when the fiction permits; attribute names and starting values remain provisional.

## 2026-09-08 — Natural-language message classification

- Use one composer for Dungeon Master questions, character actions, and in-world speech; require no mode switch, prefix, or command syntax.
- Rowan infers the message type entirely from ordinary language and context.
- `What do I know about this ward?` is a zero-time question to Rowan.
- `Inspect the ward.` is an instruction for the character to act in the world.
- `I'll ask Mara about the ward.` may be underspecified: Rowan asks whether James wants a general question or has a specific subject in mind.
- When clarification is required, Rowan preserves the player's intent, does not recommend a strategy, and commits no fictional time or state until the intended action or speech is clear.

## 2026-09-08 — Character creation as Session 0

- Character creation is a true Session 0 with Rowan, not only a configuration form.
- The player assigns stats directly from a fixed standard array similar to D&D; exact values and the final attribute vocabulary remain open.
- Rowan first asks the player to propose their own background or character concept.
- Rowan may offer background suggestions only when the player asks for help.
- Rowan asks what the player thinks would be especially cool and what they hope to experience in the game.
- Session 0 output guides story setup toward conflict and character growth while preserving simulation integrity.
- Narrative steering may establish opportunities, tensions, and ingredients, but must not predetermine outcomes, railroad the player, or override NPC agency.
- The in-play prohibition on suggested actions and recommendation chips remains intact; explicitly requested creative help during Session 0 is a separate interaction.
- Product-specific UX concern: clearly distinguish player-authored character facts, agreed campaign premises, player preferences, and non-binding narrative hopes.

## 2026-09-08 — Session 0 confirmation and personalized recognition

- Rowan opens character creation by asking the character's name.
- Rowan then asks open-ended questions: where the character came from, what they care about, and what they hate.
- During Session 0, the character sheet is the stat-assignment surface.
- Initial stat values are editable until character confirmation, then become read-only through ordinary character-sheet use.
- The character sheet also owns titles and achievements; both sections are empty for a new character.
- Before play begins, Rowan describes the character back to the player and explicitly asks whether the understanding is correct.
- A major personalized payoff is a skill upgrade named after the character or a genuinely unique achievement arising from their history.
- New exploration: retain a registry of achievements awarded across players so the game can detect and recognize situations no player has reached before.
- Open: whether first-ever recognition is player-visible, how situation identity is compared independently of generated wording, what data scope is shared across campaigns/players, and which release phase owns the feature.

### Source reconciliation

- Existing reward types are distinct: achievements award item prizes, titles grant buffs, and earned ability variants are optional upgrades; an achievement does not automatically become a skill upgrade.
- Achievements, titles, affinities, and earned ability variants are excluded from P0. Conditional P1 budgets one generated achievement/prize, one title, and one earned ability variant.
- Current persistence is save-branch-local. No global player account, cross-player event registry, world-first arbitration, or privacy/consent model exists.
- A save-local “unprecedented in this character's history” recognition fits the current design.
- Cross-player “no player has ever done this” recognition is retained as a possible later indie-release feature requiring explicit online scope and policy decisions.

## 2026-09-08 — Recognition scope confirmed

- Session 0 ends when Rowan reflects the character accurately, the player confirms it, and a personally relevant opening situation begins.
- Personalized achievements, titles, and ability upgrades arise from subsequent play rather than being granted during character creation.
- First-pass novelty is evaluated within the character/save history.
- Achievements, titles, and ability upgrades remain separate reward types.
- Cross-player world-first recognition is deferred to a possible online indie release.

## 2026-09-08 — Accessibility baseline

- The first-pass browser experience targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA conformance across complete pages and responsive variations.
- Screen-reader-friendly narration, speaker attribution, controls, status changes, and reference overlays are baseline scope.
- Retain full keyboard operation, visible unobscured focus, non-color speaker/message labels, no timed reading, and reduced-motion handling.
- Pending/resolved/failed Dungeon Master states are programmatically announced without forcing focus.
- Overlay behavior must expose an accessible name and role, provide predictable keyboard dismissal, manage focus appropriately, and return focus to the invoking control.
- Accessibility applies to generated narrative and dynamic status content as well as static UI.
- Correction: an in-game Settings surface is not required merely to duplicate browser/OS text-size and motion controls.
- Support browser text/page zoom through at least 200% without loss of content or functionality and ensure layout reflow at the WCAG 2.2 AA target viewport.
- Honor the user's system/browser `prefers-reduced-motion` preference; an additional in-game motion toggle is optional convenience, not baseline scope.
- Native browser capabilities do not replace application responsibility for semantic structure, contrast, keyboard interaction, focus management, accessible names, or live status announcements.

- Kyle confirmed that dmud must leave preferred text size to the browser rather than provide an in-game text-size control.
- Typography must use relative units and inherit the browser's root text size; the application must not replace the user's browser preference with a fixed root pixel size.
- Browser text/page zoom and narrow-width reflow remain required. Enlarging text must preserve content, controls, reading order, and functionality without two-dimensional page scrolling except for intrinsically two-dimensional content.
- No Settings surface or custom 16–24 px control is required for text sizing.
- This decision conflicts with the GDD v0.7 `G15` approved presentation target for an adjustable 16–24 px control. As the newer direct stakeholder correction, it governs the UX spine; the upstream GDD still requires correction so the source contracts agree.
